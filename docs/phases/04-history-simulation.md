# Phase 4 — Historical simulation

Status: planned, implemented incrementally in M3–M6. No implementation yet.

## Inputs and outputs

Inputs: phase 3 initial state, physical data, versioned simulation settings, seed, and an explicit stopping condition.

Proposed outputs: `final_state.json`, `events.jsonl`, `metrics.csv`, and optional versioned checkpoints. Events carry identity, occurrence time, participants, location, causal factors, effects, and links to relevant earlier events. Information delivery time is separate from occurrence time. Record actual state transitions, not a chronology invented after simulation.

## Simulation structure

Begin with a small discrete-time model. Time units and same-time ordering must be specified before implementation. A candidate tick orders arrivals/information delivery, settlement production/consumption, research completion, decisions, and departures. Resource availability and political decisions must not depend accidentally on dictionary iteration order; document how simultaneous competition is resolved.

Use stable, purpose-specific random streams or keys. Record deterministic tie-breaking and serialization. Checkpoint replay should match an uninterrupted run when that feature is added.

## M3: travel and survival

- Population, industrial capacity, supplies, and explicit units.
- Decisions: explore, settle, resupply, invest; feasible actions compete for limited capacity.
- Expeditions have origin, destination, sponsor, payload, departure/arrival time, and vessel capability.
- Physical travel distances need an explicit 2D/3D decision; current CSVs cannot recover 3D pairwise distances.
- Supply delays, settlement viability, infrastructure development, failure, and persistent abandoned sites.
- Explain action selection and important rejected alternatives without logging every arithmetic operation.

Accepted transport direction: initialize toward an 80% committed / 15% maintenance / 5% serviceable-uncommitted allocation, then track changing capacity locally by settlement. Faction aggregates are reports, not a freely available global fleet. Derive availability from location, commitments, condition and access; account for transfers and travel time without counting one vessel at multiple settlements. Demand shocks such as an ore discovery can tighten capacity; released commitments can loosen it. War termination is another user-provided future example, with military modeling still deferred. No event should directly reset capacity to the initial percentages or inform actors before relevant information arrives. Units, accounting windows and specific demand responses remain to be designed.

Accepted expedition risks: food may spoil and some cryosleep passengers may fail to wake. A nominal five-year post-arrival food allocation does not guarantee five years of usable stocks after transit. Keep an aggregate model of usable food, losses and surviving settlers; the accepted qualitative risk profile is small routine losses with occasional serious failures. Exact probability distributions, exposure intervals and severity parameters remain to be discussed. No mortality or spoilage rate has been accepted. Outcomes may affect colony viability through resource availability and labor, not an unexplained success/failure roll. Record losses and their modeled causes; distinguish the occurrence from discovery/reporting of a loss. Use stable seeded randomness and explicit stock/population accounting; no negative food, revived dead passengers or duplicated deaths across arrival/revival events.

Accepted expedition scope also includes optional human embryo stocks, cryosleep livestock and livestock embryo stocks. Track them separately from born human population and active livestock. Successful revival, gestation, birth and maturation affect capacity at different times; embryo arrival must not instantly add workers or productive adult animals. Artificial wombs/incubators are accepted for both humans and livestock. Equipment capacity, quantities, developmental delays and viability rules remain open; artificial gestation does not imply accelerated maturation. Use aggregate stocks/cohorts, with reconciled transfers and losses, rather than individual genetic simulation.

Accepted demographic direction: human children mature normally, with approximately age 18 as the adult-workforce threshold. Mortality is variable and local, responding to war, famine, extreme weather and inadequate production capacity, among other modeled conditions. Use aggregate age cohorts; reconcile births, deaths, aging and migration without creating instant workers. Baseline rates, condition effects and combined-risk accounting remain unspecified. Record causal factors and avoid counting the same death twice when pressures overlap. Represent production shortfalls through their effects on supplies, care or infrastructure rather than assuming every lost unit of production kills residents. War and weather are intended influences for later mechanics, not newly implemented systems. Planned tests should cover cohort maturation, population conservation and reproducible mortality under controlled adverse conditions.

Accepted industrial trajectory: Earth begins with most general industrial capacity, while Luna and Mars perform most spacecraft assembly. Industrial development increasingly shifts outward with colonization. Capacity changes require investment, supplies, infrastructure, labor and time; do not automatically subtract Earth capacity whenever a colony builds new industry. Outward growth can reduce Earth's share even while its absolute output grows. Accepted initial general-industry shares are Earth 80%, Luna 8%, Mars 11% and Ceres 1%; absolute capacity, costs and growth rates remain open. Ship assembly also expands outward as colonies acquire shipbuilding capability. Colonies do not automatically receive functioning yards on founding; construction, staffing and supplies must support new capacity. Accepted initial assembly shares are Luna 47%, Mars 36%, Earth orbit 15% and belt yards 2%; these evolve as capacity develops.

Accepted construction benchmarks: approximately three years for a major yard to build a large colony transport or million-ton hauler, and one year to convert a suitable existing hull for interstellar service. These assume available materials, shipyard space, labor and applicable technology. Actual delivery can be delayed by shortages; allow parallel projects only with separately available capacity. Do not double-book yard/labor resources, spend materials twice, or keep a vessel in transport service while being converted. Specific resource units and work-progress rules remain open.

Accepted allocation priority: colony survival first, with essential supplies and maintenance normally preceding new construction. Replenishing depleted emergency reserves normally comes before discretionary expansion, after immediate essential needs and maintenance. Resources beyond those priorities can go to industrial, commercial and cultural endeavors, including the arts. Government/company overrides remain possible with causal effects on shortages, equipment condition and trust. Policies cannot spend the same resources on multiple projects or assume all residual capacity is interchangeable. Numerical subsistence thresholds, reserve-replenishment rates and surplus allocation mechanisms remain unspecified. Cultural activity is a first-class intended investment category, but no automatic benefit or detailed arts simulation is yet defined.

Accepted new-colony agriculture target: a well-equipped colony reaches food self-sufficiency in about three years under favorable conditions, with gradual production growth and possible delays from setbacks. Compare actual output with current food demand, accounting for population changes, consumption, spoilage and stocks. This is a development target, not an automatic timed unlock or guaranteed survival. Specific production ramps and resource requirements remain to be defined.

Accepted early settlement preference: prioritize relatively hospitable worlds for major founding colonies; harsh-world settlements are generally smaller mining or scientific outposts. Evaluate sites from dated actor knowledge rather than omniscient physical truth. Exact site scoring, thresholds, survey requirements and outpost scale remain open; do not interpret preference as an absolute ban or turn habitability scores into unrecorded environmental facts.

Accepted mission sequence: first send a survey probe; dispatch a colony ship after viability information reaches the deciding sponsor. Early probes report by ordinary light-speed radio, explicitly revising the former ship-carried-only premise for these reports. Schedule reception after survey/transmission plus one year per light-year of physical separation; knowledge is unavailable before receipt. Radio speed is independent of propulsion level. A return flight is not required for radio delivery. Allow six months after probe arrival for the initial viability survey of habitability, resources, hazards and a landing site, followed by report propagation. Detailed surveying can continue afterward. Range/infrastructure constraints remain open. Later probes carry a communications relay plus equipment for basic automated colony setup and begin local preparation if their findings establish viability. Keep probe-local knowledge/action distinct from remote knowledge, and tie relay functionality and automation to available technology, resources and explicit mission authority. Accepted automated preparation establishes power, communications, a landing area and basic shelters; settlers still bring the main equipment and supplies needed for survival. Exact survey criteria, preparation duration, capacities, setup milestones and relay capabilities remain open. Probe setup does not automatically create an inhabited settlement or unlock FTL communications.

Accepted claim prerequisite: establish a colony, outpost or mining operation before staking a territorial claim. Surveying and basic automated site preparation alone confer no claim. Other factions may recognize or dispute the claim; establishment does not automatically produce universal recognition. Claims cover individual moons, planets or asteroids rather than entire star systems, allowing different factions to claim different bodies in one system. Use the existing composite body identity (system_id, object_id) when referencing a claimed body. An established automated mining station qualifies without resident staff, but its claim can be disputed and the station can be hijacked. Staffing or security can help protect it; neither is required to stake a claim nor guarantees protection. A takeover changes actual control without automatically granting recognized sovereignty. Exact security, hijacking and dispute-resolution mechanics remain open. Keep claims, actual control and actor knowledge distinct, with recognition or disputes dependent on received information.

Accepted abandonment and salvage direction: abandonment can eventually forfeit a territorial claim, with a grace period protecting temporary evacuations or supply interruptions. Salvage rights apply to abandoned colonies, stations, bases, outposts and similar facilities. An operating automated facility is not abandoned merely because it has no resident personnel. Keep salvage of assets distinct from territorial claims; salvaging a site alone does not establish a qualifying colony, outpost or mining operation. The grace period must allow enough time for notice to reach the owner and for a recovery expedition to reach the site, including reasonable expedition preparation. Its duration therefore depends on applicable communication and travel times rather than a universal fixed interval. An owner may explicitly relinquish a site, opening it to salvage without waiting for the abandonment grace period. Other actors can act on that relinquishment only after receiving the information. Once a site is eligible for salvage, salvagers may restore it and establish a new territorial claim when it operates as a qualifying colony, outpost or mining operation. Salvage activity alone does not qualify. The new claim covers the individual body and remains subject to recognition or dispute under the existing claim rules. The exact calculation, preparation allowance, abandonment criteria and salvage adjudication remain open. Preserve abandoned sites and their remaining assets rather than deleting them; actors must receive evidence of abandonment before acting on that knowledge.

Accepted location-control model: each visitable planet, moon, asteroid or station has one controller, or is explicitly unclaimed. Use this same abstraction in the expansion simulation and the playable map. Multiple cities or facilities may exist in the fiction, but do not become separately faction-owned settlements within the same destination. Other factions can have residents, offices, businesses and influence without sharing location control. Different locations in one system may have different controllers. Rival claims and disputes do not create multiple simultaneous controllers; control may change over time and remains distinct from recognized claims. Luna and Mars each retain a joint colonial administration as their single location controller, preserving the three discovering blocs' joint sponsorship and internal politics. This supersedes the earlier allowance for differently managed settlements on one claimed body. Exact controller representation remains to be specified; no new faction or location IDs are assigned here. Earth’s destination controller is a shared port authority, while the eight blocs remain separate political actors. Preserve their governments and political relationships; destination control does not imply a unified Earth government. Authority membership and powers remain unspecified.

Accepted trade direction: trade generally follows local supply, local demand and transport costs. Governments may prioritize essential deliveries during shortages. Such prioritization allocates available resources and shipping capacity; it does not create goods or guarantee timely delivery. Supply and demand differences between locations also attract traders from nearby systems seeking profit: they can buy from surplus locations and deliver to markets with unmet demand. Evaluate expected profit after transport costs using available shipping capacity, travel times and port access. Trade decisions use information available to the deciding actor; distant shortages or surpluses are not known instantly. Deliveries can reduce the differences that attracted trade, and conditions may change before arrival, so profit is not guaranteed. The complete commodity roster, pricing formulas and prioritization mechanisms remain under review. Accepted category names are Raw Materials (formerly Mining Output) and Refined Materials (formerly Refined Metals). Raw Materials includes ice and unprocessed gases alongside ores and other extraction products. Refined Materials includes purified water, oxygen and processed chemicals alongside refined metals and other processed materials. These inclusions do not add separate commodity categories; exact production and consumption accounting remains to be specified. Narcotics replaces Drugs as the recreational/illicit-drug category name, distinct from Medical Supplies. Alien Artifacts is retained as a tradable contraband category, providing an incentive to discover and exploit alien ruins and outposts. Artifact cargo must come from actual discoveries and recovery; hidden sites do not become actor knowledge through market logic. Recovery quantities, cargo representation, site depletion and enforcement details remain open, and trading an artifact does not automatically grant its associated technology. Black-Market Data is the accepted replacement name for Blacknet Assets.

Accepted starting trade legality: Alien Artifacts and Black-Market Data are prohibited in ordinary markets and traded through black markets. Narcotics legality varies by faction or location. Weapons require licensed trade; trading without required authorization is smuggling. Other goods are normally legal but may be subject to embargoes or emergency export restrictions. Keep legality distinct from availability, visitor disposition and port access. Exact local policies, licensing scope, detection and penalties remain to be designed; no faction-specific narcotics policies or enforcement rates are assigned here.

Accepted black-market access: black markets exist at some locations, not universally. Access requires a local contact or sufficient criminal reputation, and enforcement can disrupt the market. Keep market existence, actor knowledge and access distinct; a hidden market is not automatically visible to every visitor. Exact contact acquisition, reputation thresholds, disruption and recovery rules remain open.

Accepted capability-driven economy: settlements may combine mining, refining, agriculture, manufacturing, high-tech and services/research roles. Roles summarize actual capabilities rather than granting fixed output. Every trade good must have an underlying production, recovery or acquisition basis connected to the location's resources, environment, infrastructure, available inputs, technology and workforce. Physical conditions make industries more or less viable; population size and conditions influence both productive capacity and demand. New colonies prioritize survival needs and develop export industries as capacity permits. Advanced technology centers can support more Black-Market Data activity through technical skills and access to valuable information. Agriculture supporting medical/pharmaceutical production can also support Narcotics production. These are opportunities and tendencies, not automatic illicit output at every suitable site; local participants, incentives and conditions matter. Demand likewise reflects residents' needs and circumstances and operating industries' inputs, rather than a fixed import list attached to a location label. Distinguish local production from imported stock and resale.

Historical design references: [habitat industry profiles](../archive/game-design/Universe_Simulation.txt), resource distribution and economic interdependencies in the same file. These support the accepted design direction but do not automatically adopt archived population ranges, extraction rates, legality rules or schemas. The accepted physical dataset supplies body class, ore_richness, fuel_richness, habitability and risk, not a detailed inventory of specific minerals, gases, soils or chemical feedstocks. Capability mappings and any additional resource detail must be explicitly designed without replacing preserved physical outputs; actor decisions still require available knowledge. Commodity input/output mappings, workforce/technology requirements and numerical production and consumption rules remain open.

The working [commodity capability and demand table](03-initial-scenario.md#proposed-commodity-capabilities-and-demand) is the single proposed mapping for the twelve-category roster under review. It is not an implementation or an accepted set of production recipes. Resolve its remaining material choices before implementing those relationships.

Accepted water/ice abstraction: water and ice remain conceptually within Raw Materials and Refined Materials, without separate substance inventories or conversion recipes. A location’s resource profile and installed industrial capabilities determine viable aggregate outputs. Water recycling and purification are represented through life-support infrastructure and maintenance; failures can cause supply crises without a separate water inventory. This supersedes the proposed need to track ore-versus-ice compatibility beneath market categories. Exact capability mappings remain open; preserve existing physical datasets and accepted separate food accounting.

Accepted investment and expansion drivers: governments and private businesses may build or expand industries within local policy. Governments weigh survival and strategic needs; businesses pursue expected profits, with actual choices also shaped by political and economic circumstances. Projects require funding, materials, labor, applicable technology, infrastructure and construction time. Local and external demand, resource availability, transport/access constraints and conflict influence whether to invest, explore or exploit additional resources. The pursuit of wealth can motivate exploration and new extraction beyond immediate subsistence needs. War can redirect priorities, increase demand or disrupt resources and trade; exact military mechanisms remain deferred. Actors use information they have received, not hidden deposits or instantly known distant market conditions. Investment competes with other commitments and the accepted survival/reserve priorities; it does not guarantee profit or successful expansion. Model these influences through a manageable set of aggregate factors, with recorded causes and participants, rather than a detailed reconstruction of contemporary geopolitics. Decision weights, financing and project-selection rules remain open.

Accepted resource depletion and economic transition: extraction gradually depletes accessible deposits. Investment, exploration and discoveries can open additional deposits; depletion of an accessible deposit does not necessarily mean exhaustion of the entire body. Technology can improve extraction efficiency and access, but higher extraction throughput can accelerate depletion over long timescales. Distinguish efficiency, extraction rate and remaining resources; technology does not automatically replenish deposits. Settlements can diversify or shift their economic emphasis over time, for example from mining to manufacturing and later services. These are possible trajectories, not a mandatory progression or an automatic upgrade at a settlement age. Transitions require the relevant investment, skills, inputs, infrastructure, demand and time under the accepted investment rules. Successful adaptation is not guaranteed. Represent depletion and developed capacity in evolving simulation state linked to preserved body identities; do not rewrite the accepted physical dataset. Initial recoverable quantities, depletion rates, discovery rules, extraction-technology effects and transition mechanics remain open.

Accepted migration direction: people may migrate toward jobs and better living conditions and away from shortages, conflict or industrial decline. Migration requires available transport and permission to settle; movement takes actual travel time rather than instantly redistributing population. Decisions depend on information available about potential destinations. Track residents departing, people in transit and arrivals without double counting; migrants leave the origin workforce on departure and do not join the destination workforce before arrival. Migration changes local labor supply and consumption needs and interacts with industrial development and population conditions. Accepted migration funding options are self-funded passage, employer sponsorship for needed workers, and government-funded colonization or evacuation. Funding remains subject to the payer’s available resources and does not bypass transport capacity, settlement permission or travel time. Exact migration rates, destination choice, admission policies, fares and sponsorship terms remain open; hardship or opportunity does not automatically move every affected resident.

Accepted migration pressures and refugees: rapid immigration can strain housing, food supplies and public services even as arrivals expand the workforce. If arrivals outpace supporting infrastructure, shortages and reduced local trust may follow. War can cause mass emigration and refugee crises at nearby systems. Displacement creates demand for transport and refuge, not instantaneous population transfers or automatic admission. Preserve the distinction between attempts to flee, departure, people in transit and admission/arrival; existing transport, travel-time, information and population-accounting constraints apply. Destination admission and emergency-response policies remain to be designed. Evaluate effects from actual arrival numbers, available capacity and policy responses rather than assigning an automatic trust penalty to refugee status. Exact displacement rates, reception capacity, relief allocation and recovery mechanics remain open.

Accepted population-accounting simplification: routine ship crews are abstracted and are not tracked as separate demographic cohorts or population transfers when ships depart or arrive. Colonists and other migrants remain explicit population movements: subtract them at departure, track them in transit, and add surviving arrivals at the destination without double counting. The user’s example of 100,000 migrants illustrates a consequential transfer, not a minimum tracking threshold or a revised ship passenger capacity. Crew operating/provisioning costs may remain aggregate ship costs; this demographic simplification does not remove them or decide future player-facing crew mechanics.

Accepted population granularity: model aggregate settlement/community counts and conditions, with aggregate age cohorts where needed, never individual people. Separate populated location objects, including the Earth orbital station, have their own population totals. Population transfers operate on aggregate counts; routine ship crews remain abstracted. The Earth orbital station starts with 100,000 permanent residents. Whether they are allocated from or additional to the previously accepted Earth total remains unspecified.

Accepted default space-station template: stations import food and industrial supplies, maintain their own life-support systems and target six months of essential reserves. Apply this template to Earth’s orbital port/shipyard and as the default for other space stations. The reserve target is coverage for normal essential consumption/import needs, not six months of every commodity or every specialized spare. Initial stations assigned this template start with the reserve allocation; stations established during simulation must acquire supplies through production, delivery or founding cargo rather than receiving free stock. Six months remains a target that can change through consumption, replenishment and disruption, not guaranteed survival or a permanently full inventory. Capacity, consumption rates and the existing reserve-day convention remain to be specified; the template does not assign every station Earth orbit’s population or industrial role.

Accepted freight-market adaptation: carriers may reassign ships toward more profitable routes while honoring existing contracts and essential-supply commitments; redeployment takes actual travel time and respects available information. Supply and demand apply to transport capacity as well as cargo. Underserved settlements may pay high freight charges, establish their own fleets, or attract new companies that fill gaps. These are possible responses, not automatic relief: they require funding, access to ships through acquisition or construction, operating resources and time. The initial commercial economy includes named Meridian and KVI plus hundreds of other operators represented in aggregate. Track aggregate independent capacity locally or by service, separately from named-company assets, without treating independent operators as a centrally managed faction. Named firms are intended as recognizable actors in the eventual game and may be modeled during history generation; their continued market shares or success are not guaranteed. Two is not a limit on actual company count. New operators and settlement fleets must acquire assets through accounted transfers or construction rather than receive free ships; preserve ownership, location and commitments without double counting. Freight-rate formulas, market-entry criteria, fleet acquisition and contract mechanics remain open.

Accepted initial independent transport sizing: size aggregate independent freight and passenger capacity from local demand and service workloads using the existing 80% committed / 15% maintenance / 5% serviceable-uncommitted planning target. Count named-company ships and their actual assigned capacity toward each workload first; independent operators supply the remaining requirement, not an automatic additional fleet multiplier. Account for payload or passenger capacity, route cycles, existing commitments and location; freight tonnes and passenger berths are separate measures. Busy ports may support larger pools because of demand, not because each port receives a fixed fleet. The number of independent businesses is not a ship count. If named capacity already covers a service, do not add independent capacity without another documented demand or allocation. Exact initial demand outside the accepted cargo baselines, independent quantities and integer scheduling remain open.

Accepted passenger-accounting distinction: business trips, tourism and other temporary visits consume passenger transport capacity but do not change permanent settlement population totals. Only relocation transfers permanent residents between settlements, with departures, transit and arrivals accounted for under the migration rules. Represent routine travel as aggregate passenger flows rather than individual visitor records; do not count temporary visitors as new permanent residents. Exact temporary-trip demand, return flows and any local service-load effects remain open.

## M4: technology and knowledge

Demographic behavior for staged implementation: accepted artificial-birth planning considers food, housing, care and expected production, with unexpected crises able to invalidate plans. Governments can encourage or discourage natural births, but outcomes depend on resident response as well as policy. Distinguish natural births from artificial gestation throughput; neither bypasses gestation, childhood or population accounting. Exact rates, costs, decision timing and policy mechanisms remain unchosen. Population trust and compliance interact with M5 political behavior; do not claim these are already implemented in M3.

Every artifact-associated technology can also be developed through independent research. Sites can accelerate progress toward that technology; they are not exclusive sources or mandatory prerequisites. Discovery alone does not automatically grant mastery, deployment or shared faction knowledge. The magnitude and mechanism of acceleration remain to be designed.

Propulsion improvements increase speed and decrease travel time, never reach. The physical reachability network is fixed across technology levels. Improvements require research or discoveries and deployment through construction/refits. Old vessels do not automatically acquire new performance. Initial information channels include ship-carried messages and accepted light-speed survey-probe radio reports: source, destination, content, observation date, transmission/departure date and arrival date matter. Propulsion upgrades do not increase radio speed. Actors act only on available knowledge. Discovery, decoding, exploitation, and diffusion are different events.

Accepted initial propulsion diffusion direction: independent research, espionage, personnel movement and commercial licensing spread knowledge from or alongside the three discovering blocs to the other five, targeting broad access around 2168. This does not require exposing the secret artifact. Record actual research completions, transfers, participants and information arrival; access to knowledge is distinct from industrial capacity and deployed vessels. Exact mechanics, relative pathway importance and target enforcement remain unresolved; no particular theft, license, migration or research event has been generated.

## M5: political evolution

Sustained satisfaction/legitimacy pressures, autonomy demands, negotiation, alliances, conflict, independence, merger, and extinction. Political actions consume resources, have delays, and use inertia/recovery rules to avoid oscillation. Administrative and supply burdens counter unbounded expansion. Ownership changes do not delete settlements or rewrite cultural ancestry. Retain extinct factions for historical references.

Accepted explicit requirement: track local population trust in its current government, changing with conditions and government actions. Residents can support or resist birth policies and other decisions. Trust and satisfaction can affect cooperation and productive effectiveness; sustained discontent can lead to unrest, revolt and breakaway governments. This is not an automatic binary switch from low trust to successful independence. Specific measures, initial values, rates, thresholds and transition mechanics remain to be designed. Use causes, persistence and existing capacity constraints; high trust does not create resources or eliminate shortages. The user chose the local colony administration as the primary target of population trust; Luna and Mars can differ despite identical sponsors. Any additional sponsor-specific attitudes or blame attribution remain optional future design, not required duplicate trust systems. Hidden wrongdoing affects public trust only when information or observable consequences reach residents. Planned checks should cover policy/outcome differences, differing local trust within one faction, recovery after improved conditions and explainable secession without duplicating population.

Accepted direction: cooperation, including equal-vote Luna/Mars joint authorities, can coexist with backroom deals and attempts to undermine competitors. Private agreements, concealed sponsorship and competitive obstruction are intended political behavior, not implemented features. Specific mechanisms, costs, decision criteria, success probabilities and discovery rules remain to be designed before implementation. Do not force betrayal on a schedule or assign immutable behavior by bloc identity.

Record actual participants, commitments, costs and causes in simulation truth while exposing only information an actor has received. Distinguish suspicion from evidence and attribution; a failed project must not automatically reveal a hidden sponsor. Candidate verification scenarios should include a private deal benefiting its participants without informing outsiders, an obstructed project with uncertain attribution, and consequences after evidence reaches an affected actor. Preserve incentives to cooperate and the possibility that interference fails or backfires.

Accepted hacker-underground direction: the Mesh (also known as the Null Collective) is one decentralized movement; model it with independent participants loosely aligned around free information and opposition to concentrated power, not a single executive choosing every action. Activity is noisy and sometimes contradictory; affiliation and attribution are uncertain to observers. Use a simple aggregate or small-group representation to be chosen before implementation, without individual hacker simulation. Keep actual participants, claimed responsibility, suspected affiliation and information received distinct. Do not give every participant all movement knowledge, or make public claims automatically true. Accepted action scope includes occasional partial leaks of restricted propulsion research and politically motivated disclosures of unflattering information intended to undermine opponents. Research leaks assist progress without automatically granting complete designs, manufacturing capacity or every technology level. Participants choose sides independently; no uniform permanent allegiance is implied. Effects on trust or diplomacy depend on information reaching actors and their assessment of it. Do not preassign targets, invent false evidence or guarantee that a release succeeds. Specific starting actors, events and quantitative decision rules remain open.

Accepted corporate-colony direction: KVI may administer colonies under a sponsoring bloc's charter with substantial but bounded local authority. Such colonies are a frequent setting for its questionable business dealings. Keep charter sponsor, local corporate administration, assets and residents distinct; account for local trust and information about misconduct without prescripting crimes or automatic revolt. Accepted oversight is light in routine operation, with investigations or intervention prompted by serious complaints, exposed misconduct or threats to sponsor interests. Political protection can influence whether and how sponsors respond. Information must reach the relevant sponsor before it can act; no automatic omniscient intervention is implied. Specific powers, contractual obligations, thresholds and consequences remain to be designed.

Accepted autonomy direction: colonies can gain independence peacefully through negotiation as well as through revolt or conflict. Negotiated autonomy can be an intermediate step; specific agreements, authority transfers and transition rules remain to be designed. The user expects strong local identity in many colonies after a generation or two as ties to Earth weaken. Let identity, communication/travel separation, local conditions and political decisions influence trajectories without automatically equating identity with disloyalty or triggering independence on a fixed generational clock. Accepted: political independence does not automatically end friendly economic relations, trade, technology agreements or shared interests with the former sponsor. Track those relationships separately from sovereignty; review agreement continuity according to the actual transition rather than imposing universal cancellation or preservation. No relative frequency of peaceful versus violent independence is accepted. Record causes, participants and continuity of residents, settlements and obligations across political transitions.

Accepted port-access default: ports generally admit peaceful foreign traders. Embargoes, conflict or local policy may restrict access. Foreign affiliation alone does not imply denial, and conflict does not automatically impose a universal closure. Specific restrictions and enforcement rules remain to be designed. Preserve the distinction between a port’s actual access policy and a traveler’s knowledge of that policy.

Accepted visitor-disposition direction: track signed dispositions toward a ship or entity separately at faction and settlement level, with negative values unfavorable and positive values favorable. Evaluate the combined disposition for access and security responses, including for the player. Hostility or a very low disposition can deny access; sufficiently severe hostility can result in an attack by local security. Preserve the faction and local values independently. The combination formula, scales, denial/attack thresholds, disposition changes and security-response mechanics remain open. Attacks are possible, not mandatory at every hostile encounter; this does not define military capabilities. Visitor disposition is distinct from residents’ trust in their government.

Accepted refugee admission and social reception: receiving governments may admit refugees, limit arrivals or close ports, balancing humanitarian concerns, capacity, security and politics. Factions can differ in their responses, including welcoming or xenophobic attitudes toward outsiders. Resident populations may welcome or shun immigrants; public reception is distinct from official admission policy and can differ between settlements within a faction. These attitudes can vary with the arriving group, relationships, history and local conditions and may change over time; no ancestry or culture is assigned an immutable response. Admission alone does not guarantee social acceptance. In this human-only setting, xenophobia concerns human outsiders rather than living alien species. Specific faction defaults, representation of public attitudes and effects on integration, trust and unrest remain open.

Accepted integration direction: integration can improve over time through employment, adequate housing and fair treatment, while exclusion and prolonged hardship can deepen tensions. Integration is uneven and not guaranteed; some populations may remain socially separate, and authorities or local pressures may impose segregation, including confinement to ghettos. Distinguish cultural continuity and voluntary community formation from exclusion or coercive segregation. Cultural identity need not disappear for people to participate in local economic and civic life. Outcomes depend on policies, opportunities, public reception and lived conditions rather than an automatic assimilation timer or an inherent trait of a population. Use aggregate community-level representation; exact integration measures, segregation mechanisms and effects on trust, opportunity and unrest remain open.

Accepted community-level conditions: track trust and living conditions for population groups within each settlement, so a prosperous settlement can still contain marginalized communities whose conditions differ from its average. Groups partition or otherwise reconcile to the resident population; membership does not create additional residents or separately controlled destinations. Poorer communities may experience higher crime rates, represented as a conditional risk linked to material hardship and local circumstances, not a guaranteed outcome or a fixed cultural/ancestral attribute. Preserve the distinction between community conditions, criminal participants and the broader resident population. The user clarified the SimCity analogy as an example of emergent indirect effects: pollution affects property values, cheaper housing attracts poorer residents, and hardship can affect crime risk. This is an illustrative chain, not a request to implement property values, housing-price sorting or a direct pollution-to-crime rule. Desired secondary outcomes should follow modeled intermediate conditions rather than receive an additional duplicate effect. Community definitions, numerical crime-risk factors, reporting and aggregation rules remain open. Keep this an aggregate social model rather than an individual-person or city-grid simulation.

Accepted government responses to worsening conditions: governments may fund relief, expand housing and public services, encourage employment, adjust migration policy or increase security. Choices depend on available resources, priorities, corruption and public pressure. Responses consume resources and take time; they may fail or worsen tensions rather than automatically restore stability. Evaluate effects through the conditions and communities actually affected, preserving actor knowledge limits and causal records. Low trust alone does not automatically trigger revolt. Exact response costs, delays, decision weights, effects and escalation rules remain open; increased security does not define military capabilities or guarantee success.

Accepted unrest escalation: persistent grievances can lead to protests, strikes, organized opposition and eventually rebellion. This is not a mandatory sequence; events may skip stages or subside through negotiation and improved conditions. Rebellion requires organization and resources as well as dissatisfaction, so low trust alone is insufficient. Keep grievances, organized participants and their available means distinct, and record causes and participants for escalation or de-escalation. Exact thresholds, timing, organizational requirements and resource costs remain open; this does not establish military capabilities or a detailed combat model. Rebellion can give rise to new breakaway factions; the faction roster is not fixed to its starting members. Record a new faction’s origin, parent faction or factions, founding participants and causal events. Track faction formation, actual territorial control and recognition separately; formation does not guarantee independence, universal recognition or control of a location. Preserve one controller per visitable location, resident and asset continuity, and actual knowledge transfers without duplicating populations, assets or granting the parent’s entire knowledge automatically. Exact faction-formation criteria and succession rules remain open.

Accepted political consolidation: factions may form strong alliances or unions, or merge. Distinguish an alliance between continuing independent factions, a union with shared institutions whose member autonomy depends on its terms, and a merger into a single successor faction. These relationships and transitions complement rebellion and breakaway formation; the political map may consolidate as well as fragment. Record participating factions, agreements and causes. Preserve historical identities and resident, asset and obligation continuity; shared institutions do not automatically confer every member’s knowledge on all others. Alliances do not by themselves change location controllers, and unions must remain compatible with one controller per visitable location. Exact formation, authority-sharing, succession and dissolution rules remain open.

Accepted covert support: outside factions and corporations may secretly fund or supply opposition movements and breakaway factions to undermine rivals. Support may include money, supplies, information or weapons. Support requires actual resources and delivery or information-transfer time; it does not guarantee recipient success, loyalty or control by the sponsor. Distinguish actual sponsors and recipients from what other actors know or suspect. Exposure of involvement can damage relations or provoke a political crisis, subject to information reaching the affected actors. Record participants, transfers and motives in simulation truth without automatically revealing them in public history or player knowledge. Exact eligibility, costs, detection, attribution and reaction rules remain open; military capabilities remain deferred.

Accepted corporate bargaining motives: corporations may seek exclusive mining rights, favorable trade terms or political influence in exchange for covert support. Treat these as negotiated objectives and possible concessions, not automatic rewards for providing support. Record the parties, promised terms and any concessions actually granted; keep secret agreements distinct from public knowledge. A recipient may fail to deliver or later reject an arrangement. Exact bargaining, enforcement and breach mechanics remain open.

Accepted nationalization possibility: a change in government type may lead the government to nationalize corporate assets. This is a possible policy decision, not an automatic consequence of every government transition. Nationalization changes ownership of affected assets without creating, deleting or relocating their physical capacity, inventories or resident workforce. Identify the former corporate owner, acquiring government and affected assets, and record the decision and resulting ownership transfers. Asset ownership remains distinct from the location controller. Scope, compensation, corporate response and diplomatic consequences remain open; no universal compensated or uncompensated rule is selected.

## M6: culture and communications

Culture can persist, mix, migrate, and diverge; ownership and language remain separate. Names track original/current/alternate forms, language, naming actor, reason, and date. Start with authored, reviewable name pools; do not map ethnicity directly to aggression or intelligence.

Research or artifacts may unlock faster-than-light communication. Unlock is distinct from deployment: network reach, access, equipment, ownership, and message latency determine who benefits. Ship-carried news remains a fallback. Decide the mechanism and cost before implementation.

## Verification gates

Test causal scenarios: resource-limited settlement; home-government collapse during transit; treaty news arriving late; hidden artifact discovery; unequal technology; colony independence; culture persisting after conquest; communication outside network reach. Assert resource accounting, valid references, monotonic time, no knowledge from the future, deterministic replay, and bounded state values.

Additional planned checks: zero-risk preservation, reproducible spoilage and cryosleep-loss outcomes, bounded losses, stock and survivor reconciliation, and colony effects when delivered food or workforce is reduced. These are future verification requirements, not completed tests.

Run larger seed sweeps only after small scenarios are understandable. Political stability targets remain tunable and reviewed; avoid forcing a predetermined winner, faction count, or historical timeline.
