# Phase 3 — Initial Earth scenario

Status: scenario design in progress, M2. No implementation yet.

Initial user choices are recorded in [the decision log](../DECISIONS.md#phase-3-scenario-direction--september-2026). The confirmed starting year is 2148, with Mars discovery on April 5. The accepted scenario starts at discovery on April 5, 2148, with approximately five years of development before first operational interstellar ships around 2153. This explicitly supersedes the original pre-epoch scenario premise while preserving the accepted physical dataset. The accepted propulsion progression starts at 0.5 c and doubles speed each level, including faster-than-light speeds; relativity and time dilation are explicitly outside the model. Gameplay should eventually have journeys lasting days, around one day per light-year. Advancement timing remains Phase 4 design work.

## Purpose

Create an explicit starting state at the dawn of interstellar travel. Future political geography must emerge during phase 4, rather than be assigned in advance.

## Starting government structures after the map revision

The map revision does not itself supply new constitutions. Preserve compatible earlier institutional decisions while reopening those affected by merging blocs:

- **Atlantic Union:** accepted parliamentary federation. Member governments retain domestic authority; an elected union parliament chooses the executive responsible for shared policy and budgets. The former separate North American and Western European structures are superseded, not two current sovereign blocs. Electoral details and division of powers beyond this shared remit remain open.
- **Indo-Pacific Compact:** accepted council-led alliance of self-governing members. Governments negotiate shared trade, infrastructure and space programs; a rotating chair coordinates agreed policy. Major commitments require broad member support. Exact representation, chair terms and voting thresholds remain open.
- **Sino Cooperative Sphere:** retain the dominant communist party, central leadership council and mixed state-directed economy, with strategic state industries and smaller private/cooperative enterprises.
- **Eurasian Directorate:** retain the Russia-led strategic confederation with member governments and a coordinating council. The new name does not silently impose a different constitution.
- **Indian Confederation:** retain parliamentary democracy at the Indian core and a council of self-governing associated states, with India contributing most.
- **West Asian League:** Saudi Arabia remains the leading member of the bargaining council of monarchies and allied governments, with meaningful council representation for other major members of the expanded League. Exact membership, seats and voting weights remain open. No historical reconciliation event is invented.
- **African Union:** retain the accepted DRC leadership, member domestic authority and representative council; the new framework broadens the economic emphasis beyond mining. Higher initial institutional corruption remains the earlier accepted condition, mutable over time.
- **Southern Commonwealth:** retain the Brazil-led democratic confederation and council of elected national leaders, with higher initial institutional corruption as previously accepted.

The Belt Workers' Commonwealth retains its elected workplace/settlement councils, Ceres assembly and assembly-chosen executive. Its short name remains the Commonwealth; use Southern Commonwealth or Southern bloc explicitly for the Earth faction to avoid ambiguity. Meridian Shipping, KVI and the Mesh/Null Collective remain unchanged. Generally stable initial support is retained; detailed representation and voting rules remain open where needed.

## Accepted opening diplomatic situation

At the April 5, 2148 start, no major war is underway between the Earth blocs. Rivalry, espionage, commercial disputes and uneasy cooperation are present. Ceres's independence remains a source of tension, and piracy threatens some shipping. This does not imply universal peace, assign specific covert operations or pirate attacks, or preselect a future war. Preserve the accepted joint Luna/Mars institutions and India's friendly relationship with the Eurasian Directorate. Commonwealth independence has mixed recognition: some blocs recognize it, while former backers dispute its status but still trade with it. The former consortium backers are the Atlantic Union, Eurasian Directorate and West Asian League; under the accepted mixed-recognition arrangement, these three dispute Commonwealth independence while continuing trade. Recognition positions of the other five blocs, bilateral dispositions, active disputes and military/security capabilities remain to be specified where required by implementation.

### Discovery and technology access discussion

Preserve Mars, April 5, 2148, joint discovery, roughly five years of development before first operational 0.5 c ships around 2153, and broad diffusion of underlying propulsion knowledge by roughly 2168. These remain approximate scenario targets rather than guaranteed simulated outcomes. The earlier 2162 colony-arrival target is superseded: arrivals follow probe travel, survey time, radio report delivery, expedition preparation and colony-ship travel at its actual capability. A 2153 probe departure toward 4.32 ly at 0.5 c yields an illustrative colony arrival around 2175 only if survey/preparation add negligible time and the colony ship also travels at 0.5 c; this is not a replacement fixed target. Faster later ships can shorten that journey. The discovery does not create the already-existing blocs or established colonies.

Accepted partnership: Atlantic Union, Sino Cooperative Sphere and Indo-Pacific Compact jointly sponsor Luna/Mars and co-discover the artifact. Qualitative joint-program contributions are Atlantic research/aerospace/instruments, Sino manufacturing/large-scale construction, and Indo-Pacific shipbuilding/robotics/transport; these are nonexclusive roles, not technology grants or numerical asset allocations. The discovery is initially kept secret, and the partners closely guard what the artifact contains. Each immediately begins applying emerging findings to its own propulsion designs; this initiates development, not instant working technology. Knowing an artifact exists, possessing research data, understanding propulsion and having deployed ships are different states. The existing 2168 diffusion target does not give every bloc operational technology in 2148 or guarantee equal fleets later. The user specifies that Atlantic develops the first working prototype and publicly announces it; a first production unit follows soon afterward, with Sino and Indo-Pacific following closely. Preserve approximately 2153 as the existing operational-ship target; exact dates and intervals remain open. A propulsion announcement does not automatically reveal the artifact, its contents or reproducible designs. Accepted public account: Atlantic presents the prototype as its own domestic research breakthrough. The three partners continue concealing the artifact's existence and contents. Record this public claim separately from the actual joint alien discovery; hearing the announcement does not grant technical designs or knowledge of its origin. Accepted diffusion paths to the other five blocs are a mixture of independent research, espionage, personnel movement and commercial licensing, with broad propulsion-knowledge access targeted around 2168. Diffusion does not require public exposure of the artifact. Exact recipients, dates, agreements and relative contributions of each path remain open. Knowledge access does not automatically provide production infrastructure, ships or later propulsion levels. Later disclosure of the artifact itself remains open. Treat Atlantic-first as user-specified scenario chronology to support explicitly, not as an already simulated outcome or an inherent faction advantage. The boundary between authored milestone constraints and resource-driven Phase 4 outcomes requires an explicit implementation choice.

Public cooperation does not imply complete trust or shared private information. The user expects backroom deals and efforts to undermine competitors. Initial institutions should permit distinct sponsor interests; do not invent specific starting conspiracies. Ongoing political mechanisms belong to Phase 4.

Scientific outposts can initially be facilities within these settlement totals. Distinct orbital stations need an explicit facility/location representation; outposts on natural bodies absent from the accepted Sol catalog need a deliberate physical-world extension. Do not invent host object IDs or silently modify accepted data.

Smaller facilities on Luna, Mars and Ceres are included within those existing destinations rather than separate visitable locations. This adds an artificial destination in the planned Phase 3 scenario, not a new natural body or a modification to the accepted physical datasets. Individual blocs and corporations may own facilities within it; those holdings do not create separate location controllers. As a separate object, the station has its own aggregate population of 100,000 permanent residents, separate from Earth’s surface population. No individual residents are simulated. Its name, whether those residents are allocated from the previously accepted 6.5-billion Earth total or additional to it, specific facility ownership allocations and assigned capacity remain open. Preserve the accepted industrial and assembly totals when assigning its capacity. This decision does not establish an exhaustive roster of all possible Sol outposts.

Accepted default space-station template: stations import food and industrial supplies, maintain their own life-support systems and target six months of essential reserves. Apply this template to Earth’s orbital port/shipyard and as the default for other space stations. The reserve target is coverage for normal essential consumption/import needs, not six months of every commodity or every specialized spare. Initial stations assigned this template start with the reserve allocation; stations established during simulation must acquire supplies through production, delivery or founding cargo rather than receiving free stock. Six months remains a target that can change through consumption, replenishment and disruption, not guaranteed survival or a permanently full inventory. Capacity, consumption rates and the existing reserve-day convention remain to be specified; the template does not assign every station Earth orbit’s population or industrial role.

Accepted migration direction: people may migrate toward jobs and better living conditions and away from shortages, conflict or industrial decline. ### Establishing territorial claims

Accepted: an actor must establish a colony, outpost or mining operation to stake a territorial claim. Surveying alone or basic automated site preparation does not qualify. Establishing a claim does not guarantee recognition by other factions; claims may be recognized or disputed. A claim covers the individual moon, planet or asteroid where the qualifying operation is established, not the entire star system. Different factions can therefore claim different bodies in the same system. An established automated mining station qualifies without a resident crew. Its claim can be disputed and the station can be hijacked. Staffing or security is a prudent protective investment, not a prerequisite for staking the claim or a guarantee against takeover. Basic automated site preparation alone still does not qualify. Exact security and hijacking mechanics remain open. Preserve the distinction between corporate administration, asset ownership and sponsoring sovereignty.

Accepted abandonment and salvage direction: abandonment can eventually forfeit a territorial claim, with a grace period protecting temporary evacuations or supply interruptions. Salvage rights apply to abandoned colonies, stations, bases, outposts and similar facilities. An operating automated facility is not abandoned merely because it has no resident personnel. Keep salvage of assets distinct from territorial claims; salvaging a site alone does not establish a qualifying colony, outpost or mining operation. The grace period must allow enough time for notice to reach the owner and for a recovery expedition to reach the site, including reasonable expedition preparation. Its duration therefore depends on applicable communication and travel times rather than a universal fixed interval. An owner may explicitly relinquish a site, opening it to salvage without waiting for the abandonment grace period. Other actors can act on that relinquishment only after receiving the information. Once a site is eligible for salvage, salvagers may restore it and establish a new territorial claim when it operates as a qualifying colony, outpost or mining operation. Salvage activity alone does not qualify. The new claim covers the individual body and remains subject to recognition or dispute under the existing claim rules. The exact calculation, preparation allowance, abandonment criteria and salvage adjudication remain open.

Accepted location-control model: each visitable planet, moon, asteroid or station has one controller, or is explicitly unclaimed. Use this same abstraction in the expansion simulation and the playable map. Multiple cities or facilities may exist in the fiction, but do not become separately faction-owned settlements within the same destination. Other factions can have residents, offices, businesses and influence without sharing location control. Different locations in one system may have different controllers. Rival claims and disputes do not create multiple simultaneous controllers; control may change over time and remains distinct from recognized claims. Luna and Mars each retain a joint colonial administration as their single location controller, preserving the three discovering blocs' joint sponsorship and internal politics. This supersedes the earlier allowance for differently managed settlements on one claimed body. Exact controller representation remains to be specified; no new faction or location IDs are assigned here. Earth’s game destination uses a shared port authority as its single controller. The eight Earth blocs remain separate political actors; the port authority does not replace their governments or imply a unified Earth government. Authority membership, powers and controller representation remain unspecified.

Accepted port-access default: ports generally admit peaceful foreign traders. Embargoes, conflict or local policy may restrict access. Foreign affiliation alone does not imply denial, and conflict does not automatically impose a universal closure. Specific restrictions and enforcement rules remain to be designed. Preserve the distinction between a port’s actual access policy and a traveler’s knowledge of that policy.

Accepted visitor-disposition direction: track signed dispositions toward a ship or entity separately at faction and settlement level, with negative values unfavorable and positive values favorable. Evaluate the combined disposition for access and security responses, including for the player. Hostility or a very low disposition can deny access; sufficiently severe hostility can result in an attack by local security. Preserve the faction and local values independently. The combination formula, scales, denial/attack thresholds, disposition changes and security-response mechanics remain open. Attacks are possible, not mandatory at every hostile encounter; this does not define military capabilities. Visitor disposition is distinct from residents’ trust in their government.

### New colony food production

Accepted target: a well-equipped founding colony becomes food-self-sufficient in approximately three years after establishment under favorable conditions. ### Accepted colony resource priorities

Colony survival is the primary concern. By default, essential supplies and maintenance take precedence over new construction. After immediate essential needs and maintenance are met, replenishing depleted emergency reserves normally precedes discretionary expansion. Remaining resources and capacity can fund industrial, commercial or cultural endeavors, explicitly including the arts. Government/company overrides are possible under the accepted priority proposal, with consequences for shortages, equipment condition and public trust; do not assume that every actor always follows an optimal survival policy. Use explicit allocations without spending the same surplus twice. Exact subsistence/reserve thresholds and allocation rules remain open. Cultural investment is an intended use of resources, not an implemented happiness bonus or a predetermined cultural program.

### Accepted construction and conversion benchmarks

A major shipyard takes approximately three years to build a large colony transport or million-ton cargo hauler, or one year to convert a suitable existing ship for interstellar service, assuming materials, yard space and labor are available. Conversion also requires the relevant operational technology and an eligible hull. These are supported-work benchmarks, not unconditional delivery dates; resource shortages or unavailable facilities delay work. Multiple projects may proceed in parallel only when sufficient capacity exists for each, without double-booking resources.
